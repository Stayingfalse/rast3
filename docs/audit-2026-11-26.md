# Audit, 26 Nov 2026 (the day before gifting opens)

Asked for by the owner after the broken email links (#113): "a full audit of
the code and system to make sure nothing else is hiding. Think of outlier
cases, security and usability."

## Fixed in this PR

| Severity | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| High | **No limit on sign-in emails.** Anyone could ask for sign-in links to any address, as often as they liked; each one is a real email from noreply@raosanta.uk. A script could use up Gmail's daily allowance (about 2,000), stopping every sign-in and reminder, and get us marked as spam. | `src/server/sign-in-limits.ts`: 4 links per address in 15 minutes and 10 a day; 30 requests per network address in 15 minutes (an office shares one address); 200 an hour and 600 a day for the whole site. Over a limit, the sign-in window shows its "couldn't send" message, and a direct request lands on `/auth/error?error=TooManyRequests`. |
| Medium | **`/auth/magic` fronted any link.** The landing page put our "Click to Sign In" button on whatever address `?url=` held, so `raosanta.uk/auth/magic?url=https://evil.example` was a branded phishing page. | It only accepts our own `/api/auth/callback/...` address; anything else says "No valid sign-in link found". |

## Checked and sound

- **Links in emails and pages** (the #113 bug class): every link is built from
  the site's origin (`siteUrl()`, `new URL(AUTH_URL).origin`, or the request's
  own host). Invite links, banners, posters, the calendar file, share cards and
  `metadataBase` are all right.
- **Access control:** every procedure that takes a group, team, assignment,
  purchase or thank-you ID checks the caller may act on it: by looking it up
  with the caller's own ID, or with `requireOrganiser`, `requireTeamAccess`,
  `requireCanPickUp`, `requireGroupFeed` or `kudosModeration`. Deleting a
  thank-you needs a site admin, or the organiser of a workplace or team the
  author is in (joining one needs a verified work address).
- **Public endpoints:** only the thank-you feed is open without sign-in, and
  it shows what the home page does (group feeds check membership).
  `/api/account/export` and `/api/wishlist-reports` need sign-in; images are
  limited to `kudos/<id>`; the provider-icon fetch only takes letters, digits
  and dashes. The server never fetches member-supplied addresses.
- **Test login on prod:** `/api/test-auth/*` all answer 404.
- **Headers on prod:** HSTS (preload), a Content Security Policy,
  `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `nosniff`, a strict referrer policy.
- **Emails:** every member-supplied value is escaped; one-click unsubscribe
  (RFC 8058) is set and its token checked; reminders are claimed in the email
  log, so the two containers of a zero-downtime deploy can't both send one.
- **Wishlist links:** checked on the server against the Amazon pattern, so a
  `javascript:` or non-Amazon link can't be saved.
- **Sign-in redirects:** Auth.js's default rule (own site only) is in place.
- **Picking up a wishlist:** never your own or one you already hold, fewest
  gifts first, duplicates refused by the database, and its own request limit.
- **Dates:** every member-facing date sets the UK time zone. Five admin and
  organiser screens format dates in the browser, so they show the viewer's own
  zone (fine for a UK audience).
- **Opening at midnight:** the home page is never cached and reads the
  season's dates on each request.

## Recommendations (not done; for the owner to choose)

1. **Done (26 Nov): a test mailbox on dev** (Mailpit, `ras/mailpit`). The last three email bugs (the expired Gmail
   token, the Outlook button, the broken links) were all in the real sending
   path, which dev can't exercise: it has no email server. A small mail sink
   on Easypanel (e.g. Mailpit) as dev's SMTP server would let the e2e suite
   open real emails and click their links. This is the biggest gap.
2. **Separate `AUTH_SECRET` for dev and prod** (already logged for after the
   season).
3. **Content Security Policy:** it allows `'unsafe-inline'` and
   `'unsafe-eval'` scripts and Google Tag Manager, which the site doesn't use.
   Tightening it is worth a careful pass after the season.
4. **Pending workplaces:** a workplace organiser counts as a moderator while
   the workplace is still pending approval. Low risk (only verified colleagues
   can join), but moderation could wait for approval.
5. **Hidden thank-you photos** stay reachable by their direct address after a
   thank-you is hidden. Low risk (the address is random), but a hidden
   thank-you's photos could be served only to moderators.
